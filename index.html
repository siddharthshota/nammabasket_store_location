Where should NammaBasket open its next stores?

A mid-format supermarket chain with 6 stores in Bengaluru can fund about two more. Eight neighbourhoods are shortlisted. This tool turns demand, competition, rent, access and cannibalisation into one ranked, budget-constrained recommendation.

    How the model works, assumptions and limitations

    Demand, two ways. Wallet method: households x grocery wallet (14% of income) x organised share (rises with income) x our fair share against competitors and quick commerce (1.5 store-equivalents). Footfall method: passers-by x entry rate (12% residents, 2% commuters) x 75% conversion x average bill. The two are blended 50/50 and scaled by a calibration factor from our 6 existing stores.

    Cannibalisation. Share of new-store sales that our nearest existing store loses: 35% if next door, falling linearly to 0% at 8 km. That lost contribution is subtracted, so the model values incremental profit to the chain.

    Store P&L. 10,000 sq ft store, 21% gross margin, staff Rs 1.1 Cr, fixed opex Rs 0.6 Cr, variable opex 5% of sales. Capex = fit-out Rs 2,200/sq ft + 6 months rent deposit + Rs 0.8 Cr inventory.

    NPV. 9-year lease, 12% WACC, ramp 70% / 90% / 100%, rent +5% and costs +6% a year, catchment growth applied to sales, no terminal value.

    Decision rule. Gate (payback within limit, NPV > 0), then weighted score, then greedy pick within budget, skipping a site within 5 km of a site already picked.

    Public benchmarks. MoSPI HCES 2023-24 (urban MPCE Rs 6,996; food ~40% of spend); Census 2011 (BBMP ~8.4 million people); 2025 Bengaluru retail rents (high street Rs 176-326/sq ft/month, neighbourhood Rs 45-180); listed grocery chain productivity (~Rs 31,000-35,000 sales per sq ft per year).

    Key limitation. Locality data are synthetic and the demand model is calibrated on only 6 stores. Footfall and competitor counts must be validated with site visits, mobile-location data and a pilot before signing a 9-year lease.

    Other limits. Uniform store size; no competitor reaction modelled; cannibalisation uses straight-line distance, not travel time; quick commerce treated as a constant.
